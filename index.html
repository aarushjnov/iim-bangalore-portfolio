Rhetorical analysis and rebrand of Vijay Shekhar Sharma

Team of six · Communication

How does one founder speak to investors, students and the public, and what would a stronger personal brand look like?

The brief

Study eight texts across four audiences (stage talks, social posts, results and video or podcast) and propose a rebrand.

My role

I presented the pathos section: how he uses body words, “you” and “yaar”, and the bicycle metaphor to make a regulatory setback feel like a scrape. I also showed where the same casual tone backfired in his Ratan Tata post, which drew criticism and was deleted.

Methods

SOAPS (subject, occasion, audience, purpose, speaker) and the ethos, pathos and logos framework, with sources cited for every text.

Outcome

We found that he has no personal brand of his own. His credibility is borrowed from Paytm and from others. We proposed six strategies to move him from survivor to builder: keep the warmth, add the proof.

  • 8texts analysed
  • 4audiences compared
  • 6rebrand strategies
  • 18 of 20latest LinkedIn posts were reposts

Insights I gained

  • Emotion persuades fast, but proof is what makes a speaker believable. The warmth in his speeches needed numbers behind it.
  • One tone cannot fit every audience. The casual style that worked on stage hurt him on a tribute post, so context decides what is appropriate.
  • Building a personal brand takes more than visibility. A big following means little if the ideas are not clearly his own.

Vortex ActuaryOS

Team pitch · Business and technology

An AI operating system that takes over the routine work around an actuary’s judgment.

The problem

Actuaries spend large parts of their time collecting, cleaning, validating and reporting data before any real judgment begins.

The idea

AI agents run five operational layers (data ingestion, validation, analysis, insight and human decision) so the actuary does one expert review instead.

My role

Member of the team behind the idea and the pitch, which covered the market case, pricing and an 18-month roadmap.

Why it holds up

We chose insurance over law and healthcare on purpose. Studies show AI errors are costly in those fields, while an actuary can review the output before anything is acted on.

  • 5 → 1layers to one expert review
  • 17%+legal AI hallucination rate we designed around
  • ₹12–15Lstarting plan per team, per year
  • 3×+ROI multiple for a mid-market insurer

Insights I gained

  • A strong idea needs a clear reason to exist. Choosing insurance over law and healthcare was a business decision, not just a technical one.
  • Pricing is a story about value. Showing the time and cost an actuary saves made the price easier to justify than the product features did.
  • Plans are assumptions until a customer tests them. Our revenue figures are planning numbers, and the real learning would start with the first paying client.

Break-even analysis of IndiGo

Team of six · Business Mathematics

How many passengers does India’s largest airline need to carry before it stops losing money?

The brief

Use linear functions and IndiGo’s real FY2025–26 results to find its break-even point and see how it moves when fares, fuel or fixed costs change.

My role

Member of the six-person group that split costs into fixed and variable, built the revenue and cost functions, and wrote the report.

Methods

Revenue R(q) = p × q and cost C(q) = F + v × q, solved for the point where they meet. Scenario tests changed one input at a time.

Outcome

IndiGo needs about 110.1 million passengers to break even and carried 123.4 million, a margin of safety of 10.8%. A 10% rise in fuel prices alone cuts that margin to 4.6%.

  • 110.1Mbreak-even passengers
  • 10.8%margin of safety
  • 84.4%actual load factor, against about 75% needed
  • 44%of costs are fixed

Insights I gained

  • A simple model can explain a large company. Two straight lines were enough to show where an airline’s profit begins.
  • Fixed costs shape risk. When so many costs are fixed, a small change in fares or fuel moves the break-even point by millions of passengers.
  • Assumptions have to be stated. Our fixed and variable split was a judgement call, and the report is more honest for saying so.

A year of accounts for Bharat Electronics

Team of six · Basics of Accounting

Simulating a full accounting year for a listed defence electronics company, from opening balances to final statements.

The brief

Start from BEL’s real 2024–25 balance sheet, record 30 made-up transactions for 2025–26, and produce the year-end statements under Ind AS.

My role

Member of the six-person group that recorded the entries, made the year-end adjustments and checked that the statements agreed.

Methods

Journal entries, ledgers, straight-line depreciation, an accrual for unpaid salaries, income tax at BEL’s own 24.84% ratio, and a proposed dividend of Rs 0.90 a share.

Outcome

The simulated balance sheet balanced at Rs 43,03,591 lakh, with a profit for the year of Rs 2,37,046 lakh. These are our simulated results, not BEL’s real ones.

  • 30transactions recorded
  • Rs 2,37,046 lakhsimulated profit for the year
  • Rs 0difference between assets and liabilities

Insights I gained

  • Accounting is a system of checks. When the two sides of the balance sheet agree, it is a sign that every entry was recorded properly.
  • Every number needs a basis. We wrote the reason beneath each entry, which made the work easy to review and to correct.
  • Real statements show how a business behaves. Working from BEL’s own figures showed me what a company with no borrowings looks like, and why that matters.
